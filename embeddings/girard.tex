\section{Girard's embedding}
In this section, we present Girard's embedding, which is a method to translate intuitionistic logic into linear logic \cite{GIRARD19871}.
Jean-Yves Girard introduced this embedding to represent intuitionistic logic in a linear framework, where resources are managed explicitly.
Linear logic can be seen as a refinement of classical logic, where assumptions - or resources - are managed explicitly \cite{Troelstra1992}.
In particular, linear logic distinguishes between assumptions that should be used exactly once and those that can be used any number of times \cite{Troelstra1992}, including zero times.
However, this thesis does not focus on linear logic, but on the way Girard's embedding can be used to represent the call-by-name \lc in the modal call-by-box \lc, \lab.

In general, Girard's embedding is not limited to linear logic. It is more general technique to represent one logical system in a different framework \cite{TroelstraVanDalen1988}.
The embedding can be used to represent one logical system in another framework that has a way to manage resources or assumptions.
In the context of \lan, parameters of functions can be seen as resources that are consumed when the function is applied \cite{MARAIST1999175}.
These resources can be used as many times as needed, hence the connection with Girard's embedding and linear logic.

\label{sec:name-embedding}

The model embedding of the \lan into the \lab is given by Girard's translation, denoted as $\girard{(\cdot)}$.
Thus, $\girard{M}$ is the translation of a term $M$ from the \lan to the \lab, and $\girard{A}$ is the translation of a type $A$ from the \lan to the \lab.
The translation is inductively defined in \cref{fig:girard-translation} for both terms and types.
We also give the translation of \lterm $\bi x$ in \cref{ex:girard-translation}.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \begin{align*}
    \girard{X}                 & = X                                          & \girard{x}             & = \boxe{x} \\
    \girard{(A_1 \fa A_2)}     & = \boxt{\girard{A_1}} \fa \girard{A_2}       & \girard{(\lamb{x}{M})} & = \lamb{x}{\girard{M}} \\
                               &                                              & \girard{(M N)}         & = \girard{M} \boxi{\girard{N}}
  \end{align*}
  \caption{Girard's translation from \lan to \lab.}
  \label{fig:girard-translation}
\end{figure}

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:girard-translation}
  \textbf{\boldmath${\girard{(\bi x)}}$}
  \begin{align*}
    \girard{(\bi x)} &= \girard{(\lamb{x}{x})} \boxi{\girard{x}} \\
                     &= (\lamb{x}{\girard{x}}) \boxi{\girard{x}} \\
                     &= (\lamb{x}{\boxe{x}}) \boxi{\boxe{x}}.
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

From Girard's translation, we see that a boxed \lterm can never occur on its own.
Boxed terms are always part of an application, where the left-hand side is a term in \lab and the right-hand side is the boxed term.
Therefore, the image of Girard's translation is a subset of the \lab given by the following grammar:

\vspace{10pt}
\begin{grammar}{
		\pr{$M, N$}{$\boxe{x} \gors \lambda x.M \gors M \boxi{N}$}
	}
\end{grammar}
\vspace{10pt}

We can reason that the relation $\redwe$ collapses to $\redbn$ when we consider the image of Girard's translation.
Since application in Girard's image is always of the form $M \boxi{N}$, the right-hand side of the application can never be reduced without the use of the $\zeta$ rule.
In $\redwe$, the only closure rules allowed are $\mu$ and $\nu$. 
It is clear that the $\nu$ rule can never be applied in Girard's image as it is not possible to reduce under boxed terms.
Thus, the only closure rule that can be applied is the $\mu$ rule, and $\redwe$ collapses to $\redbn$ in Girard's image.

We give a small example to clarify everything. Consider the \lterm $\bi x$. It holds that $\bi x \redn x$ in \lan. 
\Cref{ex:girard-translation-bn} shows that $\girard{(\bi x)} \redbn \girard{x}$ also holds.

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:girard-translation-bn}
  \textbf{\boldmath${\girard{(\bi x)}}$ under $\redbn$}
  \begin{eqnarray*}
    \girard{(\bi x)} &=& \girard{(\lamb{x}{x})} \boxi{\girard{x}} \\
    &=& (\lamb{x}{\girard{x}}) \boxi{\girard{x}} \\
    &=& (\lamb{x}{\boxe{x}}) \boxi{\boxe{x}} \\
    &\redbn& (\lamb{x}{\boxe{x}}) \subst{\boxe{x}}{\boxe{x}} \\
    &=& \boxe{x} \\
    &=& \girard{x}
  \end{eqnarray*}
\end{example}  

We now discuss the properties of Girard's translation.
Girard's translation preserves the reduction and evaluation relations of \lan.
Preservation of reduction is the first proposition and is defined by \cref{prop:girard-preservation-red}.
More specifically, it states that if a \lterm $M$ reduces to $N$ in \lan, then $\girard{M}$ reduces to $\girard{N}$ in \lab.
The proof of this proposition is rather simple and is given in the chapter about the Agda formalisation, \cref{sec:proofs-main-propositions},
though it requires a helper lemma, \cref{lem:girard-pres-subst}, which states that Girard's translation preserves and reflects substitution.
The proof of this helper lemma is rather complex, because of the definition of substitution.

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:girard-pres-subst}
  \textbf{Preservation of substitution} under Girard's translation.
  \center
  For all \lterms $M$, $N$ and $x$, $ \girard{M}\subst{\girard{N}}{\boxe{x}} \equiv \girard{(M \subst{N}{x})}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:girard-preservation-red}
  \textbf{Preservation of reduction} under Girard's translation.
  \center
  For all \lterm $M$ and $N$, if $M \rednb N$ in \lan, then $\girard{M} \redbbox \girard{N}$.
\end{proposition}

The second proposition about Girard's translation is \cref{prop:girard-preservation-eval}.
It states that if a \lterm $M$ evaluates to \lterm $N$ with $\redn$ in \lan in one step,
then the translation of $M$ under Girard's embedding also evaluates to the translation of $N$ under Girard's embedding with $\redbn$ in one step.
From this proposition, it easily follows that if $M$ evaluates to $N$ by call-by-name in \lan, then $\girard{M}$ evaluates by call-by-box to $\girard{N}$ in \lab.

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:girard-preservation-eval}
  \textbf{Preservation of evaluation} under Girard's translation.
  \center
  For all \lterm $M$ and $N$, if $M \redn N$ in \lan, then $\girard{M} \redbn \girard{N}$.
\end{proposition}
