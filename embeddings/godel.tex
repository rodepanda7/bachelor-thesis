\section{Gödel's Embedding}
\label{sec:godel-embedding}

In this section, we present Gödel's embedding, introduced by Gödel in \cite{godel1933interpretation}.
Gödel introduced this embedding as a method to translate intuitionistic logic into modal logic \textsf{S4} \cite{troelstra1933f,godel1933interpretation}.
More specifically, an embedding to represent intuitionistic logic in a modal framework, where box modality, $\square$ is used to indicate provability.
That is, $\square P$ can be read as $P$ is provable \cite{troelstra1933f}.

In general, Gödel's embedding is used to represent one more restricted logical system in a stronger framework \cite{McKinseyTarski1948}.
This thesis uses Gödel's embedding to represent the call-by-name \lc in the modal call-by-box \lc, \lab \cite{espritosanto_et_al:LIPIcs:2019:10525}.
The intuition behind Gödel's embedding is that boxes are used to represent values, which are already evaluated expressions \cite{espritosanto_et_al:LIPIcs:2019:10525}.

In the model embedding of \lav into \lab, we make use of an abbreviation, which we call \raiseembn similar to \cite{espritosanto_et_al:LIPIcs:2019:10525}.
The idea of \raiseembn is to swap two \lterms, while removing a box of one \lterm. 
Its use becomes clear later in this section when we give some examples.
We define \raiseembn as follows:
\begin{flalign*}
  \hspace{20pt} \raiseemb{M} := \lamb{z}{\boxe{z}M} &&
\end{flalign*}
The model embedding of \lav into \lab is given by Gödel's translation, denoted as $\godel{(\cdot)}$.
Thus, $\godel{M}$ is the translation of a term $M$ from \lav to \lab, and $\godel{A}$ is the translation of a type $A$ from the \lav to the \lab.
We also define $\godelv{(\cdot)}$ for the translation of values and types in \lav.
The embedding $\godel{(\cdot)}$ and $\godelv{(\cdot)}$ are mutually inductively defined in \cref{fig:gödel-translation} for both terms and types.
That is, we define $\godel{(\cdot)}$ in terms of $\godelv{(\cdot)}$ and vice versa.
We also give the translation of \lterm $\bi x$ in \cref{ex:gödel-translation}.

\begin{definition}[h]
  \label{fig:gödel-translation}
  \textbf{Gödel's translation} $\godel{(\cdot)}$ from \lav to \lab is defined as follows:
  \begin{align*}
    \godel{A}   & = \boxt{\godelv{A}}                                        & \godel{V}             & = \boxi{\godelv{V}} \\
    &                                                                        & \godel{(M N)} & = \raiseemb{\godel{N}}\godel{M} \\\\
    \godelv{X}  & = X                                                        & \godelv{x}             & = \boxe{x} \\
    \godelv{(A_1 \fa A_2)} & = \boxt{\godelv{A_1}} \fa \boxt{\godelv{A_2}}   & \godelv{(\lamb{x}{M})} & = \lamb{x}{\godel{M}}
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:gödel-translation}
  \textbf{Gödel's translation of \boldmath${\bi x}$:}
  \begin{align*}
    \godel{(\bi x)} &= \raiseemb{\godel{x}} \godel{(\lamb{x}{x})}  \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\godelv{x}}} \boxi{\godelv{(\lamb{x}{x})}}  \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{x}}} \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\boxi{\godelv{x}}}} \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\boxi{\boxe{x}}}}
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

Let us now reason about the image of Gödel's translation.
From the translation, it follows that all applications in the image of Gödel's translation are of the form $\raiseemb{M'} M$, 
where both $M'$ and $M$ are terms in \lab.
Every term in the image that is not an application, is of the form $\boxi{V}$, with $V$ a value in \lab.
Thus, the image of Gödel's translation is a subset of \lab given by the following grammar:

\begin{align*}
  \begin{grammar}{
    \pr{$M$}{$\boxi{V} \gors \raiseemb{M'} M$}
  } \quad \quad
  \end{grammar}
  \begin{grammar}{
    \pr{$V$}{$\boxe{x} \gors \lamb{x}{M}$}
  }
  \end{grammar}
\end{align*}

Now, we reason that the relation $\redwe$ collapses to $\redbv$ when we consider the image of Gödel's translation.
We do this by showing that the only closure rule that can be applied on each type of term in Gödel's image is the $\nu$ rule.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item%1
  If a term in Gödel's image is of the form $\boxi{V}$, we cannot apply any reduction rule,
  since we do not allow reductions under boxed terms. Besides, $V$ is a value, which cannot be reduced.
  \item%2
  If a term in Gödel's image is of the form $\raiseemb{M'} M$, we can only apply the $\nu$ rule.
  We have that $\raiseemb{M'} = \lamb{z}{\boxe{z}M'}$, which is a value. 
  Thus, $\raiseemb{M'}$ cannot be reduced and the $\mu$ rule cannot be applied.
  The only closure rule that can be applied is the $\nu$ rule.
\end{enumerate}

We give a small example to clarify everything. Consider the \lterm $\bi x$. It holds that $\bi x \redv x$ in \lan. 
\Cref{ex:girard-translation-bn} shows that $\godel{(\bi x)} \redbvs \godel{x}$ also holds.


% To clarify, we give the embedding of $\bi x$ into \lab after which we apply $\redbv$ twice. 
% It is clear that the result obtained after $\redbv$ is equal to the embedding of the result of applying $\redv$ on $\bi x$ in the \lav, which is $\Tcbn(x) = \boxi{\boxe{x}}$.  

\begin{example}
  \label{ex:gödel-translation-bv}
  \textbf{\boldmath${\godel{(\bi x)}}$ evaluated by $\redbv$:}
  \begin{align*}
    \godel{(\bi x)} &= \raiseemb{\godel{x}} \godel{(\lamb{x}{x})}  \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\godelv{x}}} \boxi{\godelv{(\lamb{x}{x})}}  \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{x}}} \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\boxi{\godelv{x}}}} \\
                    &= \raiseemb{\boxi{\boxe{x}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\boxi{\boxe{x}}}} \\
                    &= (\lamb{z}{\boxe{z}\boxi{\boxe{x}}}) \boxi{\lamb{x}{\boxi{\boxe{x}}}} \\
                    &\redbv (\lamb{x}{\boxi{\boxe{x}}})\boxi{\boxe{x}} \\
                    &\redbv \boxi{\boxe{x}} \\
                    &= \boxi{\godelv{x}} \\
                    &= \godel{x}
  \end{align*}
\end{example}

We now discuss the properties of Gödel's translation.
Just like Girard's translation, Gödel's translation preserves reduction and evaluation.
To prove these properties, we again need a helper lemma that is similar to the one used for Girard's translation.
The helper lemma, \cref{lem:godel-pres-subst}, states that Gödel's translation preserves and reflects substitution.
The lemma is divided into two parts - one concerning substitution within values, and the other dealing with substitution in any \lterm.

\begin{lemma}
  \label{lem:godel-pres-subst}
  \textbf{Preservation and reflection of substitution} under Gödel's translation.
  For all \lterms $M$, values $V$, $W$, and variables $x$:
  \[
    \godel{M}\subst{\boxe{x}}{\godelv{V}} \equiv \godel{(M \subst{x}{V})} \mbox{ and } \godelv{W}\subst{\boxe{x}}{\godelv{V}} \equiv \godelv{(W \subst{x}{V})}
  \]
\end{lemma}

Preservation of reduction of Gödel's translation is slightly more complicated than Girard's translation.
This is because Gödel's translation makes use of the \raiseembn abbreviation, which swaps two terms.
However, this swapping takes an extra step while reducing a \lterm.
Thus, \cref{prop:godel-preservation-red} states that if a \lterm $M$ in \lav reduces to $N$ in one step, then $\godel{M}$ reduces to $\godel{N}$ in \lab in two steps.
We use the notation $\redbboxt$ for two steps reduction steps of $\redbbox$.

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:godel-preservation-red}
  \textbf{Preservation of reduction} under Gödel's translation.
  For all \lterms $M$ and $N$:
  \center 
  If $M \rednb N$ in \lav, then $\godel{M} \redbboxt \godel{N}$.
\end{proposition}

The two steps that are taken next to each other are always similar and can be merged together into one step.
Let us reason about this. 

Let us say that \lterm $P$ in \lav reduces to \lterm $Q$. We have that $P$ is of the form $(\lamb{x}{M}) V$ and thus $Q$ is of the form $M \subst{x}{V}$.
In Gödel's image, we have the following:
\[
\godel{P} \equiv \raiseemb{\godel{V}} \godel{(\lamb{x}{M})} \equiv \raiseemb{\boxi{\godelv{V}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{M}}}
\]

\begin{align*}
  \raiseemb{\boxi{\godelv{V}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{M}}} 
                  &\equiv (\lamb{z}{\boxe{z}\boxi{\godelv{V}}}) \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{M}}}   \\
                  &\redbbox (\lamb{x}{\godel{M}})\boxi{\godelv{V}}  \\
                  &\redbbox \godel{M} \subst{\boxe{x}}{\godelv{V}} \\
\end{align*}

So, we have that $\raiseemb{\boxi{\godelv{V}}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{\godel{M}}} \redbboxt \godel{M} \subst{\boxe{x}}{\godelv{V}}$.
More generally, \cref{def:redbboxts} defines the relation $\redbboxts$.

\begin{definition}
  \label{def:redbboxts}
  \textbf{Gödel's two-step reduction relation \boldmath${\redbboxts}$}.
  \begin{align*}
    \raiseemb{\boxi{N}} \boxi{\lamb{x}{M}} \redbboxts M \subst{\boxe{x}}{N}\quad \quad (\redbboxts)
  \end{align*}
\end{definition}

With this new reduction relation, we can also formulate the preservation of reduction in one step.
This is done in \cref{prop:godel-preservation-red2s}.

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:godel-preservation-red2s}
  \textbf{Preservation of reduction} under Gödel's translation with $\redbboxts$ reduction rule.
  For all \lterms $M$ and $N$: 
  \center 
  If $M \redvb N$ in \lav, then $\godel{M} \redbboxts \godel{N}$.
\end{proposition}

Preservation of evaluation is more difficult to prove than preservation of reduction.
\Cref{prop:girard-preservation-eval} states that if a \lterm $M$ evaluates to a value $V$ in \lav in any number of $\redv$ steps, 
then $\godel{M}$ evaluates to $\godel{V}$ in \lab using any number of $\redbv$ steps.
Alternatively, we can also say that if a \lterm  evaluates by call-by-value to a value, 
then the translation of that \lterm under Gödel's embedding evaluates by call-by-box to the translation of that value under Gödel's embedding.

Due to time limitations, we did not prove \cref{prop:godel-preservation-eval} in the Agda formalization.
However, a less formal proof of this proposition is given by José Espírito Santo, Luís Pinto, and Tarmo Uustalu in \cite{espritosanto_et_al:LIPIcs:2019:10525}.

\begin{proposition}
  \label{prop:godel-preservation-eval}
  \textbf{Preservation of evaluation} under Gödel's translation.
  For all \lterms $M$ and values $V$: 
  \center  
  If $M \redv V$ in \lav, then $\godel{M} \redbv \godel{V}$.
\end{proposition}
